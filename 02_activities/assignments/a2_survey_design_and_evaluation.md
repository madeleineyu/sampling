## Part A - Survey Design: 

The number of your chosen topic: `1`

Describe the purpose of your survey:
```
The purpose of my survey is to understand why there is a high turnover rate within the entry- and lower-level positions, and what changes employees would like the company to make that would improve their own satisfaction within the company.
```

Describe your target population, sampling frame, sampling units, and observational units:
```
The target population is employees in the entry- and lower-level positions across the departments within the tech company.
The sampling frame is all entry- and lower-level employee who work in one of the departments within the tech company. 
The sampling units are the departments, the entry- and lower-level position within the departments, and the individual employee who holds an entry- and lower-level position during the time of the survey. The individual employee would be the observational units.
I would use stratified sampling to divide by department within entry and lower-level positions, and then randomly select participants to survey from each stratum.
```

Your 5-10 question survey:
```
1. What is your department?
2. What is your position/role?
3. How long have you worked within the company?
4. On a scale from 1-to-9 please rate how satisfied you are with your current position/role?
5. If you rated your satisfaction as lower than 9, please explain why you did not provide a higher rating.
6. What changes within the company do you think would improve your satisfaction rating?
7. On a scale from 1-to-9 please rate how satisfied you believe your colleagues are with their current position/role?
8. If you rated their satisfaction as lower than 9, please explain why you did not provide a higher rating.
9. What changes within the company do you think would improve your colleagues' satisfaction rating?
10. How soon would these changes need to occur for your/your colleagues' satisfaction to improve?
```

## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. The sampling type is a stratified sampling design (27 strata - geographic areas).
2. The sample size was about 50,000 units (completion of 24,000 questionnaires expected). The total number of households in the survey's scope was estimated at 47,436. The actual number of respondents with usable data was 19,857 and the number of responses remained following data cleaning and processing was 16,149.
3. The target population is all individuals who are 15 years old or older, living in the ten provinces of Canada (excluding residents of institutions).
4. The sampling frame is landline and cellular telephone numbers associated with a the same address, from the Census and various administrative sources within Statistics Canada's 'dwelling' frame.
5. The survey modes are either an electronic questionnaire or CATI (computer assisted telephone interviewing) in French/English.
6. The timeline is September to December (sampling reoccurs every 5 years)
7. The overall response rate was 41.9%.
8. There are estimation weights for analysis at the person level and bootstrap weights to account for design-based variance estimation (e.g., sampling variability for all of the estimates produced based on the data). Person weight was calculated for each respondent by multiplying the household weight by the number of persons 15 years of age or older in the household (Initial household weight x Factor 1 [combination of non-response adjustments] x Number of eligible household numbers). In sum, weight adjustments were made relating to the province, volunteer status, stratum, age and sex of the respondent.
9. Data processing was done by a data capture system which both respondents and interviewers used to complete the questionnaire. The data capture program allowed a valid range of codes for each question, had built in edits, and automatically followed the flow of the questionnaire. The data output was encrypted and transmitted electronically to Ottawa. Written responses were coded into existing categories when possible, grouped into new categories, or left in 'other-specify'.
10. Daily transmissions of completed respondent survey records were combined to create the raw survey file. Prior to further processing, potential duplicate records were identified and eliminated. Non-response and out-of-scope (i.e., age-related, multiple telephone numbers from the same household) records were dropped. In some cases, missing or incorrect information was corrected deterministically or imputed from other information on the questionnaire. If the response codes to the previous questions indicated that the current question was ‘on path,’ the
responses, if any, to the current question were retained; if, however, a response was missing to the current question, it was coded as ‘Not Stated,’ i.e., 9 (99 or 999, etc.). If the response codes to the previous questions indicated that the current question was ‘off path’ because the respondent was clearly identified as belonging to a sub-population for which the current question was inappropriate or not of interest, the current question was coded as ‘Valid Skip’, i.e., 6 (96 or 996, etc.).
11. Sources of error are coverage errors due to differences between the target population and the surveyed population; some households may not have a telephone or telephone service. There were also possible errors in the grouping of telephone numbers per household, as it may be that not all of the telephone numbers that are grouped together belong to the same household. Non-response could occur at the household level and at the individual level. There may also be response errors and processing errors. The data was also affected by non-response bias, although a series of weights were implemented to account for this as much as possible. 
12. A limitation of the survey is that the proportion of the target population not included in the sampling frame (i.e., households who could not be reached or identified by telephone number were not included in the surveyed population). It is possible that this excluded population differed from the rest of the target population, biasing results.
13. Link to documentation and any additional sources used: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/45-25-0001/cat5/c33_2018.zip
```
